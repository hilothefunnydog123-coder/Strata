MolDX: Comprehensive Genomic Profiling for Advanced Solid Tumors (L38045)

Coverage Indications, Limitations, and/or Medical Necessity

This policy addresses comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP) performed on a patient's tumor specimen to identify somatic alterations that inform treatment selection. CGP is considered reasonable and necessary when all of the following conditions are met.

  1. The patient has advanced (Stage III or IV) or metastatic solid tumor cancer at the time the test is ordered, where metastatic is defined as spread beyond regional lymph nodes.
  2. The test is ordered by the treating physician managing the patient's cancer, and the results will be used to guide subsequent management decisions.
  3. Repeat testing with the same assay is permitted when there is documented disease progression, a new primary cancer, or to assess the emergence of therapy resistance mechanisms.
  4. The laboratory performing the test is CLIA-certified and the specific test has been registered in the MolDX DEX Diagnostics Exchange and has been assigned a Z-code identifier.
  5. The ordering physician must document, prior to ordering, how the test result will be used in the patient's treatment plan.

Technical Assessment

Analytical Validity

The laboratory must establish and document the analytical validity of the assay. At minimum, the analytical validity submission must demonstrate an analytical concordance of at least 95% against an orthogonal validated method across the reportable range of variant types.

The limit of detection must be established and reported for each variant class, and specimen adequacy criteria, including minimum tumor nuclei content, must be documented.

Clinical Validity

Clinical validity must be established through peer-reviewed published evidence demonstrating that the alterations reported by the assay are associated with the clinical condition or with response to a specific therapy. Evidence limited to analytical performance alone is not sufficient to establish clinical validity.

Clinical Utility

Clinical utility must be demonstrated through prospective studies with clinical outcomes as the endpoint. Retrospective analyses and change-in-management studies alone are not sufficient to establish clinical utility under this policy.

Limitations

The following uses are considered not reasonable and necessary and are therefore non-covered.

  • Comprehensive genomic profiling for cancer screening in asymptomatic individuals is not covered.
  • Testing performed solely to assess hereditary (germline) cancer risk is addressed by separate policy and is outside the scope of this determination.

Background

Next-generation sequencing has transformed the characterization of solid tumors over the past decade. A growing body of literature describes the prevalence of actionable alterations across tumor types, and numerous targeted therapies have received regulatory approval with companion diagnostic requirements. This background is provided for context and does not itself establish coverage criteria. Readers are referred to the cited literature for a fuller discussion of the evidence landscape.
