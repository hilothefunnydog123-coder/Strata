Comprehensive Genomic Profiling and Circulating Tumor DNA in Solid Tumors

Clinical Policy Bulletin Number: 0352

Policy

Medically Necessary

Aetna considers comprehensive genomic profiling of tumor tissue medically necessary for members with advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer when all of the following criteria are met:

  • The member has a diagnosis of Stage IIIB or IV non-small cell lung cancer; and
  • The member has not previously undergone comprehensive genomic profiling for the same tumor; and
  • The results will be used to select FDA-approved targeted therapy or to determine eligibility for a clinical trial.

Experimental and Investigational

Aetna considers the use of circulating tumor DNA (liquid biopsy) assays for treatment response monitoring or for minimal residual disease detection experimental and investigational because the clinical utility of this application has not been established in prospective studies.

Aetna considers comprehensive genomic profiling experimental and investigational for cancer screening in asymptomatic persons.

Background

Comprehensive genomic profiling refers to the use of next-generation sequencing to interrogate a broad panel of cancer-associated genes in a single assay. A substantial literature has accumulated describing the analytic performance of these panels and the frequency of actionable findings across tumor histologies. The discussion that follows summarizes representative studies and is provided for educational context; it does not constitute the coverage criteria stated above.

Several observational studies have reported that broad panel testing identifies additional targetable alterations relative to single-gene testing. Interpretation of these findings is complicated by heterogeneity in study design and endpoints, and randomized evidence linking broad profiling to improved survival remains limited.
